Oar Kit · Assembly & Mounting

Assemble your oar kit.

Assemble each sensor in its housing, secure both mounts to the oar, then use the guided motions to align them. The same kit works with iOS and Android recording.

Parts for each sensor

Lay out the parts below. Repeat the same assembly for your second sensor.

1Fit the two clips

Start with the empty black housing. Hook the retaining flange of one yellow clip over the matching housing edge, then press the clip fully home. Fit the second clip on the opposite side, with its flange toward the housing.

Check that both clips are seated and their long strap slots are unobstructed.

2Fit the ring and sensor

Stretch the flexible ring around the outside edge of the sensor. Keep it even around the sensor rather than twisted or bunched at a corner.

Insert the sensor through the open back of the housing. Press it in evenly so the button and logo face remain visible through the front opening. Check that the ring stays seated as the sensor goes in.

3Add the curved saddle

The saddle goes on the curved back of the housing, between the sensor and the oar. The housing and saddle have matching curves, so they nest flush together. The concave outer face rests against the shaft. The snap lips face the housing, away from the shaft.

Engage one edge lip, then gently flex the opposite edge until it snaps into place. Check that both lips are engaged, the saddle sits flush with no gap, and the sensor remains seated.

4Strap it to the oar

Place the saddle against the shaft and wrap the strap around the oar. At each yellow clip, feed the strap through the slot from the inside (the oar side) to the outside. Fold each end back onto the strap on that same side and press it firmly onto itself.

The strap has hooks and loops together on one face; the other face is smooth. Keep the smooth back against the oar, then fold each end back without twisting so the two fastening faces meet.

The mount should stay put when you gently try to slide or rotate it. Check it again after feathering and squaring the oar.

Position and direction

  • Place one sensor between the handle and the oar button, and the other on the blade side of the button. Keep mounts clear of the oarlock and your hands.
  • Orient both sensors along the shaft with the offset sensor button toward the blade. That is the sensor’s −Y direction. The sensor’s +Y direction points toward the handle. With the oar held vertically, blade down, +Y points upward. Keep the button face visible.
  • Use the actual sensor positions in your oar details and stiffness setup. Moving a sensor changes the setup.

Check orientation before alignment

Match the sensor axes to the blade.

Square the blade first. With the oar shaft approximately horizontal and the blade face vertical, fit both mounts with the sticker and button faces upward. Keep the offset buttons toward the blade. This puts the sensor faces approximately parallel to the horizontal drive/bend plane.

What RZ means

RZ is rotation about the sensor’s Z-axis. Z points out through the sticker face, perpendicular to the drive/bend plane. Rotation about that axis follows the oar’s sweep and bending in that plane. Feathering and squaring rotate about the shaft, approximately the Y-axis. The sign of drive rotation differs between port and starboard; the arrows below identify the axes, not a universal positive-drive direction.

Bare sensor: find the button end

Look straight at the sticker with the button nearer the bottom: +Y is up, +X is right, and +Z comes toward you. On this Ritmo sticker, the yellow curve points toward +Y; the lettering and offset button are nearer −Y.

Assembled mount: keep the same reference

The ring, cage and clips do not change the sensor axes. Keep the button and sticker visible. The USB-C port is hidden by the ring and mount hardware, so use the button end to check direction once assembled.

On the oar: face up with the blade squared

Use the same orientation for both sensors: one on each side of the oar button/collar, with +Y toward the handle and −Y toward the blade. Wrap and secure the hook-and-loop strap (Velcro) as shown in the assembly steps. Keep the mounts clear of the oarlock and your hands.

  • Blade squared: both sticker faces point up; neither mount is turned a quarter-turn around the shaft.
  • Buttons toward blade: +Y points toward the handle on both sensors.
  • Mounts secure: the saddle’s concave face rests against the shaft; its snap lips engage the housing, away from the shaft.
  • Then align: feather and square to confirm the mounts do not slip, enter the actual sensor positions, and perform the motion alignment. The sensor faces turn with the oar when it feathers.

Before the first workout

Confirm the active oar name, side, sensor identities and sensor positions. Save the motion alignment and the stiffness calibration for that setup, then make a short recording to check both sensors. Repeat alignment and review the stiffness setup whenever you move a sensor.

A phone used for boat motion needs a secure hull mount. Follow the iOS recording guide or Android phone-motion guide for the data to record.
