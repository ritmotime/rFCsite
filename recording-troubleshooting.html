Before your next row

Section link
  1. Power the sensors and check their charge. In Connect Oar, choose the correct saved oar or pair and confirm every expected sensor is connected.
  2. Check that both mounts stay secure when you gently try to slide or rotate them, and when you feather and square the blade.
  3. In Oar Settings, confirm the side, oar type, dimensions, sensor positions, alignment and active calibration factor.
  4. Secure the phone so it moves with the hull. Check Bluetooth, motion and location permissions, then make a short recording before a longer session.

A sensor is missing or keeps disconnecting

Section link

Check the connection, not just the name. A sensor shown as Found has been detected nearby; Connected means fresh readings are arriving. Check power, bring the phone closer, and confirm another phone or app is not using the sensor. Power-cycle the missing sensor if needed, then reconnect.

At a busy dock, similar Bluetooth names can belong to another boat. Verify your saved oar and its actual sensors. For a new setup, move away from other active sensors while identifying your pair.

One oar uses two sensors. A named two-oar pair uses four distinct sensors; each oar keeps its own alignment and calibration. Confirm all expected connections before recording. A partly connected pair is not evidence that both oars were recorded completely.

Curves have gaps, sudden jumps or incomplete strokes

Section link

Inspect the affected part of Session Overview, then look at nearby Individual strokes in Boat & Oar Motion curves. Note whether the interruption affects one sensor, one oar, or all signals. Check for a loose mount or an interruption in the connection; a picture of an odd curve alone does not establish the cause.

If interruptions seem to coincide with the blade-side sensor going underwater, record that observation and share it with support. The timing is a useful clue, not proof that water caused the loss. Avoid changing the sensor location without updating its measured position and repeating alignment.

When comparing technique, use portions with supported, continuous measurements and keep any exclusions clear. An average can hide problems present in individual strokes. Missing data should not be interpreted as zero force, zero speed or a completed stroke.

Motion is visible but bend, force or power is missing

Section link

Motion and loading need different inputs. Seeing movement from a surviving sensor does not establish that both sensors provide the paired measurements needed for a valid bend curve. Calibrated moment, work and power also need a suitable stiffness scale; estimated handle force needs the correct lever arm.

Check Available metrics & curves in the report and read the reason for any unavailable result. Keep any valid recorded signals for review, but do not assume one surviving sensor can reproduce every output or the missing oar. Recovery depends on what was actually recorded.

For two-oar workouts, inspect the port and starboard results separately where available. ZIP size and the presence of an animation are not reliable checks of whether both oars were recorded completely.

Directions, curves or loading look wrong

Section link

Check the saved Port/Starboard side, Scull/Sweep type, sensor identities and their physical locations. With the blade squared and shaft approximately horizontal, both sticker/button faces should point upward and both offset buttons toward the blade. Follow the mounting guide rather than inferring a sensor's location from its Bluetooth name.

If a mount moved, rotated or was replaced, repeat Oar Settings → Align Sensors, calculate and save the alignment. Alignment establishes the common sensor reference; stiffness calibration supplies the loading scale. One does not replace the other.

If loading values seem inconsistent, check the active Moment/Bend Factor, its source and units, the current inboard/outboard, and the sensor positions measured from the blade tip. A manual factor may still override a calculated factor. Do not tune calibration simply to force a preferred power number.

On Cloud Analysis, review Recorded oar settings for the affected workout. Changing the active oar today does not change the setup captured with an earlier recording.

Check units and the height reference

Section link

Pin above water means the neutral oarlock/pin reference height above the water surface. A value stored as 0.35 m is 35 cm; enter 35 in a field labelled cm. The upload form's fallback is not a measurement of your boat.

The animation's Pin height adjustment is a separate visual adjustment. Likewise, a pin-height motion curve shows displacement from its reference, not the static pin-above-water dimension.

BBtip / bottom blade tip and blade-tip depth curves use their stated geometric reference. A negative value can mean deeper relative to that reference; it does not by itself measure immersion below the actual water surface. Check the tooltip and the blade geometry before interpreting apparent digging.

Lengths can be stored in metres and displayed in centimetres. 0.13 m = 13 cm and 0.15 m = 15 cm. Blade-offset fallbacks differ between the iPhone/report and upload form; use the value saved for your workout and your actual measured geometry.

Boat motion, speed or the GPS route is missing

Section link

Boat motion needs a phone recording that follows the hull. A phone in a pocket, on a sliding seat, or loose in the boat also measures movement unrelated to the hull. Secure it to the hull or a fixed structure and keep it there throughout the session.

Check motion and location permissions before the next recording, and keep the matching phone-motion, GPS and oar files from the same session together. Oar data alone does not supply a GPS route or every boat metric.

A blank map background can be an internet issue: background tiles need a connection. A missing recorded route or a gap in GPS positions is a separate issue. The report cannot recreate positions that were not recorded. GPS speed is speed over ground, so current can affect it even when the oar effort feels similar.

Keep the original and get useful help

Section link

Keep the original complete workout export, including matching oar, phone-motion, GPS and setup information. Do not trim, rename or edit its internal recording files while investigating. A screenshot or summary CSV cannot replace the original sensor recording.

When asking for help, include:

  • The workout date and approximate time of the problem.
  • Whether you expected one or two oars, and which side was affected.
  • Which sensors showed a connection and whether any mount moved or went underwater.
  • A screenshot of the affected view, your app/report version and any availability message.
  • The original workout export, shared directly with support if requested.

A workout can include a GPS route and personal information. Share it with support through an appropriate private channel, rather than posting it publicly.
