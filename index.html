MEASURE THE OAR YOU HOLD

Oar based vs oarlock based systems: why measuring the oar matters

The oar connects your hands to the water: you hold the handle, and the blade grips the water. ritmoForceCurve™ measures bend and motion on that same oar, linking the load you apply to the sweep that turns it into work.

Contact between the sleeve and oarlock can vary as load builds and releases. Clearance can let the oarlock rotate ahead of—or continue beyond—the shaft at the catch and finish. Oar based measurements follow the shaft itself, without counting that extra oarlock travel as extra oar movement. That gives a more direct view of how you load and move the oar through the water.

Oar Kit · Assembly & Mounting

Assemble your oar kit.

Assemble each sensor in its housing, secure both mounts to the oar, then use the guided motions to align them. The same kit works with iOS and Android recording.

Assembly stepsMount on the oariOS: align & recordAndroid: align & recordMounting video

Parts for each sensor

Lay out the parts below. Repeat the same assembly for your second sensor.

1Fit the two clips

Start with the empty black housing. Hook the retaining flange of one yellow clip over the matching housing edge, then press the clip fully home. Fit the second clip on the opposite side, with its flange toward the housing.

Check that both clips are seated and their long strap slots are unobstructed.

2Fit the ring and sensor

Stretch the flexible ring around the outside edge of the sensor. Keep it even around the sensor rather than twisted or bunched at a corner.

Insert the sensor through the open back of the housing. Press it in evenly so the button and logo face remain visible through the front opening. Check that the ring stays seated as the sensor goes in.

3Add the curved saddle

The saddle goes on the curved back of the housing, between the sensor and the oar. The housing and saddle have matching curves, so they nest flush together. The concave outer face rests against the shaft.

Engage one edge lip, then gently flex the opposite edge until it snaps into place. Check that both lips are engaged, the saddle sits flush with no gap, and the sensor remains seated.

4Strap it to the oar

Place the saddle against the shaft and wrap the strap around the oar. At each yellow clip, feed the strap through the slot from the inside (the oar side) to the outside. Fold each end back onto the strap on that same side and press it firmly onto itself.

The strap has hooks and loops together on one face; the other face is smooth. Keep the smooth back against the oar, then fold each end back without twisting so the two fastening faces meet.

The mount should stay put when you gently try to slide or rotate it. Check it again after feathering and squaring the oar.

Position and direction

  • Place one sensor between the handle and the oar button, and the other on the blade side of the button. Keep mounts clear of the oarlock and your hands.
  • Orient both sensors along the shaft with the offset sensor button toward the blade. That is the sensor’s −Y direction. The sensor’s +Y direction points toward the handle. With the oar held vertically, blade down, +Y points upward. Keep the button face visible.
  • Use the actual sensor positions in your oar details and stiffness setup. Moving a sensor changes the setup.

Oar Kit · Sensor Direction

Place both sensor buttons toward the blade.

Use the offset button to tell which way the sensor should face along the shaft. Then let the motion alignment establish the sensor reference for your oar.

Sensor Y-axis direction

The round button is offset within the housing opening. Fit the sensor so that this button sits closer to the blade end of the oar.

  • Both sensors face the same way along the shaft. Sensor +Y points toward the handle; −Y points toward the blade. With the oar held upright, blade down, +Y points upward toward the handle. The offset button is on the −Y, blade-facing side.
  • The button and sensor face remain visible through the housing.
  • The saddle sits against the oar and the strap prevents the mount from rotating or sliding.

Repeat alignment if either sensor is removed, turns in its mount, or moves to a different position.

Finish with motion alignment

The mounting check gets the sensors facing the right way. The alignment recording uses deliberate oar movements to account for the remaining mounting angles and resting sensor offsets.

Row with the iOS beta.

Connect your saved oar, record the whole workout, and review the same session on your phone or in Cloud Analysis.

These instructions describe the current iOS beta workflow. Update your installed beta for the latest recording and alignment steps. Recording on Android? Use the separate Android guide.

1. Choose and connect the oar

In Oar Sensors, use Scan oar label if the oar has a QR label. Otherwise select your saved oar or choose Setup new oar. Confirm the name, side, type and sensor pair, then connect both sensors. Enable Autoreconnect if you want the pair to reconnect next time.

Check the active Moment/Bend Factor (MBF, N·m/°). Use a measured stiffness calibration, a suitable existing Cperm with the current dimensions, or an explicitly chosen manual MBF estimate. Bend shape and timing come from the aligned sensors; calibrated moment, work and power need the correct scale.

Enter the oar dimensions and sensor positions even when using a manual factor. The recording retains those values for analysis.

3. Mount the phone and start the workout

Secure the iPhone to the boat so it moves with the hull. Keep it fixed throughout the session. Allow the required Bluetooth, motion and location permissions; phone motion and GPS provide the boat-motion and speed inputs.

Review Workout Settings and the Auto Piece Timer, choose a live view, then tap Start Session. Record through the work and recovery intervals. Stop the session when the whole workout is finished.

4. Upload the whole workout

  2. Choose the saved workout and review its recorded oar and analysis settings.
  3. Tap Upload and Run Analysis. The app packages the available oar, phone, watch and GPS recordings with the saved setup.

What the workout keeps

New recordings retain full-rate oar acceleration (AX/AY/AZ, including gravity) alongside the aligned gyro data, plus fresh magnetic packets when magnetic recording is enabled. Each oar’s dimensions, sensor positions, stiffness calibration, manual inputs and applied factor are captured with the workout, including when a manual factor is used.

The server shows the captured values under Recorded oar settings, separately from editable analysis settings. Changing the active oar later does not rewrite an earlier workout. Older recordings cannot gain acceleration or magnetic samples that were never saved.

Two oars on one phone

In Two-oar setup, enable Track two oars and assign two saved oars, with two sensors each. Tap Connect both oars. Each oar uses its own alignment, calibration and reference.

Choose either oar, Both or Average in the rowing view. For sculling, the individual readings describe each physical oar and rower work/power add both. For two sweep rowers, My oar and Teammate’s oar may be on the same or opposite sides; summaries show mean work/power per rower, with the measured total labeled separately. Heart rate belongs to My oar only.

Average curves use matched strokes; unmatched strokes remain available individually. A usable shared sync event can refine timing. Otherwise, timing uses sensor arrival and assumes equal Bluetooth delay.

Magnetic angle testing

Under experimental settings, open Magnetometer & catch angles. Record magnetometer & angle references is enabled by default at 2 requested polls/second, adjustable from 1–10. Check the fresh-packet diagnostics: a requested polling rate is not a guaranteed sensor sample rate.

Advanced metrics include Magnetic catch angle, Magnetic finish angle, Magnetic reference sweep arc and Perpendicular position in drive. Perpendicular is 0°; catch is normally negative and finish positive on either side. Estimates settle after the stroke and recovery, and missing references show a dash.

These are experimental iOS estimates requiring alignment and a supported boat-heading reference. Saving and uploading the magnetic data preserves it for later analysis; the latest server recording update does not introduce a new magnetic catch-angle calculation.

  2. Enable Autoreconnect if you want the app to reconnect to that oar’s saved sensors.
  3. Allow a few seconds for powered sensors nearby to connect.

Step 3

For a new setup, scan for your sensors

Step 4

Keep the pair associated with the oar

  1. Confirm that both expected sensors are connected to the selected oar.
  2. Enable Autoreconnect for convenient connection on future sessions.
  3. Finish the setup and return to the Home screen.

If needed

Resolve an incorrect selection

  1. Stop and check which saved oar is selected.
  2. If a sensor has been assigned incorrectly, disable automatic reconnection while correcting the selection.
  3. Disconnect the incorrect sensor, then scan and select the correct one through the oar setup workflow.
  4. Save the corrected setup and re-enable Autoreconnect.

Do this away from other active sensors if possible. Closing another app or disconnecting another phone may be necessary before the sensor becomes available.

Ready

Check the setup before rowing

  1. Confirm the correct oar name and all expected sensor connections on the Home screen.
  2. Open Oar Settings and review Active Oar Details: side, type, dimensions, sensor positions, alignment and active calibration factor.

Troubleshooting

Only one sensor connects:
Check its power, move the phone closer, and scan again. Power-cycle the missing sensor if needed.
Other sensors are nearby:

The app keeps searching:
Check the selected oar, sensor power and Autoreconnect. Open the connection screen to inspect the saved pair.
Another phone or app was connected:
Disconnect there or close that app, then scan again in RitmoForceCurve.

iOS · OAR SETUP

Align your oar sensors.

Four core movements, followed by two optional steps. Work through the series with the sensors attached to the oar you will use.

This is the updated six-step iOS beta flow. Update your installed beta if it still shows the older separate waterline-hold screen.

Before you start

  1. Choose the correct saved oar in Connect Oar Sensors and connect both of its sensors.
  2. Check Port/Starboard, Scull/Sweep and the mounting positions in Oar Settings.
  3. Open Sensor Alignment for that oar. Start and finish each recording using the app’s prompts.

Keep the mounts secure. Moving recordings do not need a still pause at either end. Use the optional steps when convenient; you can skip them within the same series.

The six steps

  1. Bias · required

    Keep the blade feathered and the oar completely still for 10 seconds. Rest it on a stable support if possible.

  2. Feather and square · required

    Keep the shaft pointing in one direction. Feather and square the blade 5–6 times.

  3. Feathered level sweep · required

    Keep the blade feathered and the shaft approximately level. Sweep back and forth through at least 90° for 5–6 repetitions.

  4. Square level sweep · required

    Keep the blade square and the shaft approximately level. Sweep back and forth through at least 90° for 5–6 repetitions.

  5. Square swoop · optional

    Keep the blade square. Sweep back and forth while raising and lowering it in a smooth oval, 5–6 times. Keep it clear of the water.

  6. Squared float · optional

    Square the blade, let it float on the water, and keep the oar steady for 12 seconds.

    The float reference is saved with the oar settings and included in the workout upload.

Keep the blade clear of the water during the level sweeps. Follow the app’s calculate/save prompts to retain the alignment with this oar.

What to save and when to repeat

Check the saved alignment belongs to this oar and sensor pair. Repeat alignment if a sensor moves, rotates or is replaced. A matching QR label can supply saved alignment, but check that the mounts and sensor pair still match.

The squared float is part of this series; you do not need to add a second float capture afterward. Check the reference again when the oar or boat setup changes. If you use a separately measured manual waterline in Workout Settings, that manual value takes priority; review it before relying on the float reference.

Alignment establishes the sensors’ common reference. Stiffness calibration supplies the scale for moment, work and power.

Keep recording and analysis together

The saved alignment, oar settings and available float reference travel with the workout. The iOS gyro recording already has its mounting alignment applied. Re-analysis uses that recorded setup; choosing another active oar later does not replace the old recording’s alignment.

Detection needs confirming strokes. A piece’s final endpoint may move back to its last qualifying portion once rowing falls below the trigger or stops.

Other settings worth checking

  • Boat Class identifies the boat in the workout.
  • Speed Cutoff filters inactive periods in applicable summaries.
  • Groups collect similar strokes from anywhere in the session; pieces remain continuous intervals. Adjust group count, rate separation and metric weights when needed.
  • Smooth Stroke Metrics smooths supported metrics while responding to changes in effort.
  • Include Twist and Include Oar Depth enable those optional calculations before recording.
  • Curve display chooses when the stroke curve is shown; recording and completed metrics continue in every mode.

GPS pace describes movement over the ground. PowerSplit is a power-derived pace equivalent; wind, current and boat conditions can make the two differ.

Oar settings and water reference

Side, type and Moment/Bend Factor come from the selected oar. Check the source and any override in Oar Settings. The recording captures the applied scale and saved dimensions.

The current alignment series includes an optional 12-second squared float. Its saved reference travels with the oar and workout. Where a manual waterline below pin (cm) is entered, measure vertically from pin to water surface with the boat level; positive means below the pin. Manual entry takes priority until cleared. Check the reference again after rigging or boat loading changes.

Changing settings for future workouts does not alter a session already recording.

Energy estimates

Work-based calories use measured work and the selected efficiency assumption. Use Two equivalent oars only when one measured oar represents half of your work; follow the separately labeled two-oar summaries when recording both.

Heart-rate calories require recorded heart rate, body mass and VO₂max. They are a separate estimate and are not added to work-based calories. Missing inputs remain unavailable.

Calibrate Oar Stiffness

A known-weight calibration measures Cperm, the shaft’s stiffness constant. The app uses Cperm, the selected shaft model and the current oar dimensions/sensor positions to calculate Moment/Bend Factor (MBF).

Alternatively, enter a Cperm already measured for this shaft, or enter a manual MBF estimate. Dimensions alone cannot determine stiffness. Matching PowerSplit to a familiar effort is an estimate, not a measured calibration.

Known-weight calibration → · Dimensions and sensor positions →

Which value takes priority?

PrioritySourceWhat happens
1Manual Moment/Bend FactorThe entered MBF is used directly. Cperm and dimensions do not calculate the factor; saved inboard still determines handle force.
2Manual CpermThe entered stiffness constant and current dimensions calculate MBF.
3Measured CpermThe selected calibration’s stiffness constant and current dimensions calculate MBF.

An active direct MBF override stays in charge until turned off, even after saving a new calibration. The stored calibration and dimensions are retained. Check the displayed value, source and units before recording.

What the units mean

Moment (N·m) = MBF (N·m/°) × bend (°). Perpendicular handle-tip force (N) is moment divided by inboard length (m). Work adds positive moment × forward sweep in radians. Power divides drive work by the whole stroke cycle, including recovery.

Current iOS and report controls display Moment/Bend Factor, N·m/°. Older files may store FBF in J/degree²; the software converts that legacy value by 180/π. Do not convert a field already displaying N·m/° again.

Bend, moment, work and power →

When the setup changes

For the same shaft and shaft model, a measured Cperm can be reused after changing length, inboard/outboard or sensor positions: update the dimensions and recalculate MBF. Repeat sensor alignment when a mount moves or rotates. A different shaft needs a suitable stiffness value.

Keep the dimensions filled in even with a manual MBF. New workouts capture the oar settings, calibration source and applied factor, and show them on the server under Recorded oar settings. Editing an analysis does not overwrite that recording snapshot.

Choose a readable plot

  • Horizontal axis: select the supported axis and sweep range. The sweep plot’s left edge stays at 0°.
  • Curve appearance: white uses a thick solid bend trace, solid speed and dotted feather/square; color uses the same weights with solid colored lines.
  • Compare previous stroke: the previous trace is faint.
  • Show variability: mean curves can show ±1 standard deviation where at least two strokes support a sample.

Bend and oar speed use separate labeled axes with aligned zero lines. Bend scales to 1.3 times the previous peak, with a minimum upper limit of 0.5°. Live traces can be revised when the stroke finishes; completed boat/experimental curves remain visible through the next drive.

The variability band describes differences between strokes, not calibration accuracy or a confidence interval.

LOAD · MOVEMENT · EFFORT

Rowing Physics

Bend tells us how the oar is loaded. Motion tells us how that load is carried through the stroke. Together they give us moment, work and power.

Picture a rower at the starting line

The blade is squared in the water. The rower begins to pull, and the shaft bends before the oar has swept through much angle. The rower feels the load and the sensors see the bend, but little sweep work has accumulated yet.

As the oar rotates through the drive, that load is carried through an angle. Work now adds up. A short, sharp peak and a longer, steadier pull can reach the same maximum moment while producing different amounts of work.

Holding an oar under load without further sweep adds no sweep work, although bending the shaft can store elastic energy. Boat speed is a separate outcome: it also reflects the hull, water and wind, and movement of the rower. An oar-work measure and a GPS-speed measure therefore describe different parts of the row.

Moment: force with a lever arm

The moment curve describes the turning effect of the load about the pin, in newton-metres. The same moment can come from a smaller handle force acting through a longer inboard, or a larger force acting through a shorter inboard.

Moment (N·m) = perpendicular handle force (N) × inboard (m)

To express moment as perpendicular handle-tip force, divide by the inboard length in metres:

Handle-tip force (N) = moment (N·m) / inboard (m)

For example, 300 N·m with a 1.0 m inboard corresponds to 300 N of perpendicular handle-tip force. With a 1.2 m inboard, the same moment corresponds to 250 N. This describes the force component perpendicular to the shaft.

Moment/Bend Factor

Stiffness calibration supplies the Moment/Bend Factor for your oar and sensor positions. It converts the measured bend, in degrees, into moment about the pin.

Moment (N·m) = Moment/Bend Factor (N·m/°) × bend (°)

Changing this factor changes moment, work and power magnitudes. The curve timing remains the same. Use the factor for the active oar, its sensor positions and the recorded setup.

The current app displays Moment/Bend Factor (MBF) in N·m/°. A server field labelled Saved bend factor (FBF, J/°²) expects the older coefficient: FBF = MBF × π/180. Conversely, MBF = FBF × 180/π. Normal app uploads carry the recorded calibration; convert only when manually entering a value into a field with the corresponding label and units. Recorded dimensions remain relevant even with a manual factor.

Work adds load across the drive

Work depends on both moment and the angle travelled while that moment acts. Integrate moment against sweep angle in radians; the area under a moment-versus-degree graph needs the degree-to-radian conversion.

Drive work (J) = ∫ moment (N·m) × dθ (radians)

Stroke-average power (W) = drive work (J) / full stroke-cycle duration (s)

The full cycle includes the drive and recovery. Dividing only by drive time would give a different, higher value: mean drive power. A tall peak alone does not establish higher work or stroke-average power.

These calculations describe the measured oar and its calibration. Do not silently double one oar’s result or treat it as total crew power. Handle-tip force also depends on the saved inboard length; use the actual pin-to-hand lever arm when interpreting force at a different grip position.

PowerSplit, available under Advanced, translates effort into an erg-style equivalent using the power-to-speed relationship. It is separate from GPS pace and does not predict the boat’s speed. The former power-speed and power-distance choices have been removed from ordinary report controls.

Oar and oarlock measurements use different references

The oar transfers load between the rower’s hands and the blade working in the water. ritmoForceCurve™ measures shaft bend and rotation to describe that load and the sweep through which it acts. Combining moment with shaft movement ties work and power to the oar’s own motion.

Oar based systems and oarlock based systems use different measurement references. Contact between the sleeve and oarlock can change as load builds and releases. As clearance opens and closes, their angles can differ. Measuring the shaft avoids counting that relative oarlock travel as additional oar sweep.

Near the catch, the oarlock can rotate ahead of the shaft. As contact is taken up, a small change in shaft speed may appear. Catch miss measures shaft sweep before sustained loading, so it is not interchangeable with an oarlock system’s slip.

At the finish, the oarlock can continue rotating after the shaft has slowed or reversed. This extra travel can contribute to a larger oarlock based wash value than the oar based finish wash + release travel. Different event thresholds also affect the comparison. Measuring at the shaft keeps these metrics tied to the oar’s movement—the same oar held by the rower and loaded by the water.

Three views of boat movement

SignalWhat it describesHow to read it

Boat acceleration is transformed using the phone’s orientation so gravity is separated from horizontal motion. Boat pose, pin motion and blade depth combine sensor information with geometry and modelling assumptions. Blade slip is experimental

The experimental slip calculation compares estimated blade motion with boat motion. It depends on synchronized oar and boat recordings, oar geometry, a catch-angle reference, and the assumed current when relating ground motion to water motion. The supplied server still uses its stated assumed sweep placement; retaining new acceleration and magnetic files does not itself add server magnetic catch-angle reconstruction. Errors in any of these can shift its size or sign.

This is a developing estimate, not a validated measurement of boat speed through the water. It is also different from catch miss, which describes oar sweep before sustained loading. Use the source curves and assumptions when exploring a slip result; do not treat it as a direct blade-connection score.

Make curves easy to compare

  • Choose time or sweep angle. Time locates events in seconds; angle locates them along the oar’s sweep. Drive and recovery remain distinct.
  • Choose the phase and catch position. Show drive, recovery or both where supported; use Catch left, Catch center or the position control.
  • Adjust each metric’s range and position. Auto fits relevant visible data; All uses the broader range. Hover labels retain the metric’s own units.
  • Keep scales steady between strokes. Use Fit this stroke for a closer look or Reset plot to restore the initial view.

Equal screen height does not mean equal physical magnitude. Fused boat speed retains absolute ground-speed values; its reference line can show the complete-cycle mean rather than zero.

Understand averages and stored detail

Original server group and piece averages use the full analytical samples. Strokes are compared at matching elapsed times or sweep positions; their durations are not stretched to make every endpoint coincide. The number of contributors can change along a curve.

Groups created in the browser use individual traces stored in that HTML. Their averages can be approximate when individual curve detail has been reduced, while their metrics use the stored analytical results. Show variability describes stroke-to-stroke spread, not sensor accuracy. Density shading shows sample overlap where included.

Optional individual-sensor GZ diagnostics are available only as preset group/piece averages. The HTML does not store individual sensor GZ traces, so it cannot make new user-group GZ averages or member overlays for them. The ordinary oar-speed curve remains available.

Read the route and replay in context

The GPS track can follow a selected group or the visible session strokes. Choose a metric for colour, then zoom or pan. Map backgrounds require internet access and coverage varies. GPS gaps remain gaps; ground speed is affected by current.

The animation is a hull-and-blade model driven by available data. Use exaggeration to see small movements, then return to 1× to judge their size. A second displayed oar can be mirrored from one recording.

Show blade slip trail adds a dashed inner trail at the nearest submerged blade edge or wet shaft centreline. It responds to the waterline, depth, feather and hull motion. It illustrates assumed geometry; it does not measure actual blade immersion or water speed.

A steady reference that preserves each stroke’s movement

The updated analysis uses a slowly changing neutral hull plane from gravity directions averaged over complete neighbouring strokes. Its default window is 20 seconds. Pitch and set retain their movement within the stroke while slower shifts in the reference are removed.

The phone axes are not automatically the boat axes. Use a known signed bow direction and correct phone-to-pin offsets when available. Otherwise, automatic direction inference uses the rowing pattern and remains an estimate.

This is a rowing-relative reference, not an independent hull-level calibration. Slow real trim changes and slow mounting/reference drift cannot be distinguished without a known hull datum. The local reference removes them together. Earth-up remains separate for gravity subtraction; instantaneous drive acceleration is not treated as the neutral level.

Under the server’s boat settings, Local gravity is the current default and Whole session permits a legacy comparison. The reference window can be set from 5–120 seconds. These are processing settings: re-analyse to change them. Reference support does not bridge sensor gaps.

Updated iOS and server processing use the same neutral-reference method. Live iOS estimates can only use observations already recorded; final app refinement and server analysis can use more neighbouring data. Their results can therefore differ around boundaries, gaps and later corrections.

Compare size and timing together

Speed swing and set range are Core metrics. Pitch range, heave excursion and mean set are Advanced choices. Ranges describe the difference between high and low values within a stroke; smaller movement does not automatically mean faster rowing.

Compare at similar rate and effort. Look at when acceleration changes relative to catch and release and whether a pattern repeats over several strokes. Fused ground speed adds faster detail when inputs support it; it does not measure speed through water.

Course and turning

Drive and recovery turn are Advanced choices, split at the measured feather/release event. Select native hull turn or course-relative turn through the shared turning control.

Straight course uses the piece endpoint line. Curved course follows a fitted gradual path. Signed course offset, RMS offset and estimated extra ground distance depend on that reference and sufficient supported GPS travel.

Steering, a bend in the course, wind and unequal oar action can create similar turns. Boat motion alone does not identify their cause.

Waterline and the inner wetted edge

When the recording supplies an explicit physical waterline height relative to the pin, the updated server can use that reference and its context. A manual physical reference takes precedence over a measured one. Without an explicit height, the default animation estimates a waterline that puts one-third of the blade’s vertical height under water at the Y-catch reference, averaged across supported catches.

The app’s new optional squared-float step saves an oar reference and carries it in the upload. That saved reference is distinct from the physical waterline-height input. Until the report explicitly identifies it as applied, do not assume the server has used the float measurement to set blade depth or waterline.

Adjust Waterline offset to explore the model. Enable Show blade slip trail to see the existing outer trace and a dashed inner trace. The inner trace follows the submerged hatchet edge nearest the handle; when the shaft enters the water, it follows the shaft centreline to the surface intersection. The current marker is labelled Inner blade or Wet shaft.

The trail changes with depth, feathering, set, pitch, heave, waterline and visual exaggeration, and breaks across dry or unsupported intervals. It illustrates geometric contact, not measured blade immersion, a fluid wake or water speed. The water texture is a ground-speed cue, not a current measurement.

Experimental slip

Slip compares modelled oar motion with available boat-speed information. It requires synchronized recordings, geometry, an angle-placement reference and an assumed current when interpreting motion relative to water. The report still uses a simplified rigid-oar model.

Average drive slip is signed longitudinal slip over sufficiently supported intervals: positive means rearward, and negative values are retained. Missing sections are not filled with zero. Signed displacement can decrease; cumulative rearward travel in Diagnostics cannot.

Slip in metres per second differs from catch miss, which is angular travel before sustained load. Use slip as an experimental comparison under its stated assumptions, not a calibrated measure of blade connection or boat speed through water.

Before drawing a conclusion

  • The phone remained rigidly attached to the hull.
  • The forward direction, clock alignment and neutral-reference setting are credible.
  • The selected strokes have the phone, oar and GPS coverage needed for the chosen signal.
  • Explicit oar lengths, rig geometry and phone-to-pin offsets match the setup.
  • The report identifies which waterline and oar-angle reference it actually uses.
