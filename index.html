MEASURE THE OAR YOU HOLD

Oar based vs oarlock based systems: why measuring the oar matters

The oar connects your hands to the water: you hold the handle, and the blade grips the water. ritmoForceCurve™ measures bend and motion on that same oar, linking the load you apply to the sweep that turns it into work.

Contact between the sleeve and oarlock can vary as load builds and releases. Clearance can let the oarlock rotate ahead of—or continue beyond—the shaft at the catch and finish. Oar based measurements follow the shaft itself, without counting that extra oarlock travel as extra oar movement. That gives a more direct view of how you load and move the oar through the water.

Oar Kit · Assembly & Mounting

Assemble your oar kit.

Assemble each sensor in its housing, secure both mounts to the oar, then use the guided motions to align them. The same kit works with iOS and Android recording.

Assembly stepsMount on the oarAlign & record

Parts for each sensor

Lay out the parts below. Repeat the same assembly for your second sensor.

1Fit the two clips

Start with the empty black housing. Hook the retaining flange of one yellow clip over the matching housing edge, then press the clip fully home. Fit the second clip on the opposite side, with its flange toward the housing.

Check that both clips are seated and their long strap slots are unobstructed.

2Fit the ring and sensor

Stretch the flexible ring around the outside edge of the sensor. Keep it even around the sensor rather than twisted or bunched at a corner.

Insert the sensor through the open back of the housing. Press it in evenly so the button and logo face remain visible through the front opening. Check that the ring stays seated as the sensor goes in.

3Add the curved saddle

The saddle goes on the curved back of the housing, between the sensor and the oar. The housing and saddle have matching curves, so they nest flush together. The concave outer face rests against the shaft.

Engage one edge lip, then gently flex the opposite edge until it snaps into place. Check that both lips are engaged, the saddle sits flush with no gap, and the sensor remains seated.

4Strap it to the oar

Place the saddle against the shaft and wrap the strap around the oar. At each yellow clip, feed the strap through the slot from the inside (the oar side) to the outside. Fold each end back onto the strap on that same side and press it firmly onto itself.

The strap has hooks and loops together on one face; the other face is smooth. Keep the smooth back against the oar, then fold each end back without twisting so the two fastening faces meet.

The mount should stay put when you gently try to slide or rotate it. Check it again after feathering and squaring the oar.

Position and direction

  • Place one sensor between the handle and the oar button, and the other on the blade side of the button. Keep mounts clear of the oarlock and your hands.
  • Orient both sensors along the shaft with the offset sensor button toward the blade. That is the sensor’s −Y direction. The sensor’s +Y direction points toward the handle. With the oar held vertically, blade down, +Y points upward. Keep the button face visible.
  • Use the actual sensor positions in your oar details and stiffness setup. Moving a sensor changes the setup.

Oar Kit · Sensor Direction

Place both sensor buttons toward the blade.

Use the offset button to tell which way the sensor should face along the shaft. Then let the motion alignment establish the sensor reference for your oar.

Sensor Y-axis direction

The round button is offset within the housing opening. Fit the sensor so that this button sits closer to the blade end of the oar.

  • Both sensors face the same way along the shaft. Sensor +Y points toward the handle; −Y points toward the blade. With the oar held upright, blade down, +Y points upward toward the handle. The offset button is on the −Y, blade-facing side.
  • The button and sensor face remain visible through the housing.
  • The saddle sits against the oar and the strap prevents the mount from rotating or sliding.

Repeat alignment if either sensor is removed, turns in its mount, or moves to a different position.

Finish with motion alignment

The mounting check gets the sensors facing the right way. The alignment recording uses deliberate oar movements to account for the remaining mounting angles and resting sensor offsets.

Four distinct motion blocks

Use an open space with room to move the oar and keep the blade clear of the water. Keep the same sensor pair, positions and strap tension for alignment and rowing.

  1. Bias · rest still for 10 seconds

    Rest the oar on stable supports so both sensors are motionless. This gives a reference for their resting offsets.

  2. Feather and square · 5–6 times

    Keep the shaft pointing in one direction. Rotate between feathered and square without sweeping or lifting the shaft.

  3. Feathered sweeps · 5–6 times

    Keep the blade feathered and the shaft approximately horizontal. Sweep back and forth through at least 90°, without rolling the shaft.

  4. Square sweeps · 5–6 times

    Keep the blade square and the shaft approximately horizontal. Make the same back-and-forth sweeps through at least 90°, without feathering.

Pause for a few quiet seconds between blocks, and do only one type of motion at a time. On iOS, follow the app’s recording prompts. On Android, keep the recording running from the initial still period through all four blocks.

What the alignment does

The app or Cloud Analysis identifies the different movements, compares how both sensors respond to the same oar motion, and places their readings in a common reference. The still period helps measure resting gyro offsets. This reduces the effect of small differences in mounting angle on the calculated bend and motion.

Alignment and oar stiffness calibration serve different purposes: alignment establishes orientation; stiffness calibration supplies the scale used for moment, work and power.

When to repeat it

Repeat alignment after moving, rotating or removing either sensor, changing the sensor pair, or switching to a different oar setup. Confirm Port/Starboard and Scull/Sweep before recording. Review any setup-change warning instead of assuming a saved alignment still applies.

Save the alignment with your oar

  2. Check the setup. In Oar Settings, confirm the oar name, Port/Starboard and Scull/Sweep.
  3. Record each movement. Open Align Sensors and choose a movement from the Movement selector. Tap Start, perform the movement, then tap Done — save recording. Record Bias, Feather and square, Feathered level sweep and Square level sweep. Keep both sensors secure throughout.

  5. Start your workout. Return to the rowing screen and start the session with the intended oar selected.

  1. Verify live data first. On Main, gently move the oar with both sensors secured and confirm the values change.
  2. Start recording. Tap the red record circle. Start a few seconds before rowing so the file includes a clean beginning.
  3. Keep the app active while recording. The timer means recording is running. Avoid disconnecting Bluetooth or force-closing the app during the row.

Find, export, and upload the recording

  1. Open saved recordings. Tap the folder icon on Main, then open the folder named with the date/time of the row. The date/time naming makes the correct recording fairly easy to identify.
  2. Use the .txt file. The text file contains the data table with time, DeviceName, Acc, Gyro, Angle, magnetic field, quaternion, temperature, version, and battery columns.
  3. Check that both sensors are present. In a two-sensor recording, the DeviceName column should include both of your sensor IDs somewhere in the file.
  4. Export/share the file from the app. Open the date-named recording folder, select the .txt file, and use the share icon to send it to yourself, save it to cloud storage, or move it to the computer used for upload.
  5. Upload both TXT files the first time. Include the short alignment recording and the larger rowing recording in the same upload. You can select the files together in the uploader, or put both in one ZIP. The original TXT files are fine; no manual column deletion or splitting is needed.

Common problems

  • No sensors appear: make sure the sensors are on and close to the phone, then tap refresh. Android may also require Bluetooth and location permission for BLE scanning.

  • Values are not changing: return to the connection screen and reconnect the sensor before recording.
  • Several nearby sensors appear: use the last 3–4 characters/digits of your sensor addresses that you wrote down at home so you only connect to your own sensors.
  • Finding the recording: tap the folder icon in the app and use the date/time-named recording folder. Then share the .txt file directly from the app.

Connecting Oar Sensors

Use this page to connect the RitmoForceCurve iOS app to the two Bluetooth oar sensors before a row. The safest routine is: power the sensors, connect them deliberately, turn on Autoconnect, then leave the Bluetooth menu.

Important before a busy dock or regatta: in the iOS RitmoForceCurve app, you do not need to remember which Bluetooth name is the shaft sensor or sleeve sensor, and you usually do not need to write down sensor names or MAC/address endings. The easiest way to avoid connecting to someone else’s sensors is to connect your two sensors at home, or away from other rowers, then turn Autoconnect on before pressing DONE. After that, the app will look for that saved pair the next time those sensors are powered on.
What Autoconnect does: once your two sensors have been selected, turn Autoconnect on before pressing DONE. Autoconnect is used both to connect only to sensors that were paired/saved earlier and to automatically reconnect the next time the app is opened with those sensors powered on. Writing down the last 3–4 address characters/digits is mainly useful for Android users using the WitMotion app, not for normal iOS RitmoForceCurve Autoconnect use.
Step 1

Start from the Home screen

  1. Turn on both oar sensors before opening the Bluetooth connection screen.
  2. Open RitmoForceCurve.
  3. If the status says Sensors Not Connected, tap Connect Oar Sensors.
  4. If the status says Searching for Previous Sensors for more than a few seconds, also tap Connect Oar Sensors and do a clean connection.

Do not start a rowing session until the Home screen says Sensors Connected.

Step 2

Use a clean start when pairing or when things seem confused

  1. In the Bluetooth menu, start with Turn on scanning for surrounding devices turned off.
  2. Start with Autoconnect turned off.
  3. If any individual sensor switch is already on and you are not sure it is yours, turn it off before continuing.

This prevents the app from grabbing an old saved pair or a nearby sensor before you have confirmed the two sensors you want.

Step 3

Turn scanning on and connect your two sensors

  1. Turn on Turn on scanning for surrounding devices.
  2. Wait for the two WT901BLE67 sensors to appear.
  3. If possible, do this first setup at home or away from other sensors, so only your own two sensors are visible.
  4. Turn on the switch for each of your two sensors.
  5. Both sensors should show Connected.

The app just needs the correct two sensors. You do not have to decide which one is shaft or sleeve from the Bluetooth name.

Step 4

Turn Autoconnect on before leaving the Bluetooth menu

  1. After both sensors show Connected, turn Autoconnect on.
  2. Then tap DONE.

Turning Autoconnect on at this point tells the app to save this pair. Next time, with these sensors powered on, the app will search for the saved pair instead of asking you to pick from every nearby Bluetooth sensor.

Next time

Normal launch after a saved pair exists

  1. Turn on the two oar sensors first.
  2. Open RitmoForceCurve.
  3. The Home screen may say Searching for Previous Sensors while the app looks for the saved pair.
  4. Wait a few seconds. If the sensors are on and nearby, the status should change to Sensors Connected.

If it keeps searching, tap Connect Oar Sensors and repeat the clean-start process above.

Ready

Confirm connection before rowing

  1. Look for Sensors Connected on the Home screen.
  2. Go to Oar Settings to check the active oar, side, alignment and stiffness calibration. Follow the current iOS alignment guide if either sensor has moved.
  3. When everything looks correct, tap Start Session.

Troubleshooting

Only one sensor appears:
Turn scanning off and back on, move the phone closer, or power-cycle the missing sensor.
Wrong sensors are nearby:
Move away from other sensors if possible, turn Autoconnect off, connect only your two sensors, then turn Autoconnect back on before DONE.
It keeps searching:
Open Connect Oar Sensors, turn scanning and Autoconnect off, then reconnect both sensors and turn Autoconnect back on before DONE.
Another phone/app was connected:
Close the other app or disconnect the sensors there, then scan again.

Workout & Auto Timer Settings

These settings control how the app interprets rowing data, detects pieces, summarizes workout history, and adjusts oar-derived power values.

Auto Piece Timer

The Auto Piece Timer, or APT, is an algorithm that automatically determines when the rower is doing a piece versus taking rest strokes. This lets a separate piece timer start and stop without the rower pressing start, ready, or stop during the workout.

In the current app, the trigger can be based on Rate, Power, Both, or Either. The older description based only on stroke-rate threshold still applies when APT Trigger is set to Rate, but the new app also supports power-based piece detection.

The algorithm confirms the start of a piece after repeated qualifying strokes. It ends the piece retroactively when strokes no longer meet the trigger criteria or when the inactivity time exceeds the Auto Piece Time Cutoff, so the inactive time is not counted as part of the piece.

APT Settings

SettingWhat it does
APT Trigger Chooses how the app decides that a piece has started. Options are Rate, Power, Both, and Either.
Rate Threshold The stroke-rate threshold used when APT Trigger includes rate: Rate, Both, or Either.
Power Threshold The power threshold used when APT Trigger includes power: Power, Both, or Either.
Min Distance Removes very short detected pieces from the piece list and workout summary. This is useful for excluding short warmups, drill segments, or accidental starts.
Auto Piece Time Cutoff The amount of inactivity time used to determine that a piece has ended. The logic works retroactively so the inactive time is not counted as part of the piece. The current algorithm also ends a piece after repeated non-qualifying strokes.
Piece Rounding Choose whether pieces are summarized by distance or time.
Distance Rounding When Piece Rounding is set to Distance, choose the distance increment used to round piece summaries.
Time Rounding When Piece Rounding is set to Time, choose the time increment used to round piece summaries.

Workout Settings

SettingWhat it does
Boat Class Sets the boat class used in workout metadata and titles. Current options include 1x, 2x, 2-, 4x, 4-, 4+, and 8+.

Sharing Views, Settings & Calibrations

RitmoForceCurve includes separate sharing tools for live workout views, workout settings, and oar calibrations. The sharing method depends on what you are trying to move to another phone, rower, or boat.

Share custom views and workout settings

From the Home screen, tap Share / Import Views / Settings. The app then asks whether you want to share or import.

  1. Choose Share Views / Settings.
  2. Select which saved custom views to include.
  3. Choose whether to include current workout settings.
  4. Optional: enter a short prefix. The prefix is added to imported view names on the receiving phone, which can help identify a coach/team/rower source.
  5. Tap Generate Share Text.
  6. Use Share or Copy to Clipboard to send the generated text.
This method is text-based. It is intended for custom view layouts and workout settings, not for oar calibration records.

Import custom views and workout settings

  1. From the Home screen, tap Share / Import Views / Settings.
  2. Choose Import Views / Settings.
  3. Paste the shared text into the import box.
  4. Tap Read Import Text.
  5. Choose whether it is OK to import views, import settings, and make imported views active.
  6. Complete the import after reviewing the preview.

Imported view names can include the sender’s prefix. This helps avoid confusion when your own views and someone else’s shared views are both present.

Share oar calibrations

Oar calibrations are handled separately from live view/settings sharing because calibrations contain oar geometry, sensor locations, calibration values, and Moment/Bend Factor information.

  1. Open Sensor Mount / Oar Calibration.
  2. Go to Calib - manage.
  3. Use Export CSV to share calibration records as a file, or Import CSV to bring them onto another phone.
  4. Use Create Label PDF to print labels for selected calibrations.
  5. Use Scan Label to read a QR label and add or update a saved calibration.

Label PDFs are useful when a calibration belongs to a specific oar. The printed label can stay with the oar, and another phone can later scan the QR code to recover the calibration.

Data & metrics

Oar effort, speed over ground, timing and your body each tell part of the story. Explore the metrics by what they measure.

Oar based

Load, motion and effort measured from the oar.

MetricUnitsMeaning
PowerSplitmin:sec / 500 mEffort-equivalent split from power: 500 × (2.80 / power)^(1/3), for positive watts. It is not GPS pace or a prediction of boat speed.
PowerWDrive work divided by the complete stroke cycle, including recovery.
Drive workJPositive calibrated moment integrated through forward sweep angle. Negative load contributes no work; missing load samples are treated as zero and retained in diagnostics.
Mid work%Position at which half the drive work has been accumulated, as a percentage of loaded stroke length. Below 50% is more front-loaded.
Stroke length°Sweep covered from a sustained load threshold crossing to unloading; the end is capped at the feather threshold if load persists.
Catch miss°Sweep from the motion reversal at zero to sustained load engagement. Default bend threshold: 0.1°; default confirmation window: 0.25 s.
Finish wash°Sweep between unloading and the feather threshold (15° by default from the drive roll baseline). It can be negative if feathering begins before unloading; it is zero when the stroke end is capped at feathering.
Release travel°Sweep from the feather threshold to the end-of-drive sweep reversal.
Total sweep arc°Total forward sweep from its zero reference to maximum sweep. Catch miss + stroke length + finish wash + release travel describe this arc.
Max rotation speed°/sPeak handle-side sensor rotation speed during the drive, when that sensor can be identified reliably.
Drive acceleration°/s²Increase in handle rotation speed from the post-catch engagement dip to peak rotation speed, divided by the time between them. This start point is separate from the true Y-split catch.
Mean drive rotation speed°/sAverage forward sweep speed through the drive.
Mean recovery rotation speed°/sAverage sweep speed during the recovery.

Curves and variability

Moment shows positive load through the drive. Rotation speed includes drive and recovery. Feather/square shows the blade-orientation transition on a normalized display scale. Twist and oar depth can be omitted when not available.

Group and piece curves are averages. Standard-deviation shading shows the spread of recorded strokes around the average. Density shading shows where more recorded strokes or samples overlap. Both describe variation within the recording rather than estimating sensor accuracy. Oar depth is an orientation estimate.

Moment/Bend Factor and smoothing

The Moment/Bend Factor converts measured bend into moment about the pin. An override changes moment and effort-derived values, including drive work, power, PowerSplit and power distance. Recorded GPS speed, stroke angles and catch/finish timing are independent of this factor.

Power, rate, handle peak speed, drive acceleration and GPS speed can be smoothed for clearer trends, with faster response to substantial changes. Display smoothing does not replace a valid alignment or calibration.

LOAD · MOVEMENT · EFFORT

Rowing Physics

Bend tells us how the oar is loaded. Motion tells us how that load is carried through the stroke. Together they give us moment, work and power.

Picture a rower at the starting line

The blade is squared in the water. The rower begins to pull, and the shaft bends before the oar has swept through much angle. The rower feels the load and the sensors see the bend, but little sweep work has accumulated yet.

As the oar rotates through the drive, that load is carried through an angle. Work now adds up. A short, sharp peak and a longer, steadier pull can reach the same maximum moment while producing different amounts of work.

Holding an oar under load without further sweep adds no sweep work, although bending the shaft can store elastic energy. Boat speed is a separate outcome: it also reflects the hull, water and wind, and movement of the rower. An oar-work measure and a GPS-speed measure therefore describe different parts of the row.

Moment: force with a lever arm

The moment curve describes the turning effect of the load about the pin, in newton-metres. The same moment can come from a smaller handle force acting through a longer inboard, or a larger force acting through a shorter inboard.

Moment (N·m) = perpendicular handle force (N) × inboard (m)

To express moment as perpendicular handle-tip force, divide by the inboard length in metres:

Handle-tip force (N) = moment (N·m) / inboard (m)

For example, 300 N·m with a 1.0 m inboard corresponds to 300 N of perpendicular handle-tip force. With a 1.2 m inboard, the same moment corresponds to 250 N. This describes the force component perpendicular to the shaft.

Moment/Bend Factor

Stiffness calibration supplies the Moment/Bend Factor for your oar and sensor positions. It converts the measured bend, in degrees, into moment about the pin.

Moment (N·m) = Moment/Bend Factor (N·m/°) × bend (°)

Changing this factor changes moment, work and power magnitudes. The curve timing remains the same. Use the factor for the active oar, its sensor positions and the recorded setup.

Oar based vs oarlock based systems: why measuring the oar matters

The oar transfers load between the rower’s hands and the blade working in the water. ritmoForceCurve™ measures shaft bend and rotation to describe that load and the sweep through which it acts. Combining moment with shaft movement ties work and power to the oar’s own motion.

Oar based systems and oarlock based systems use different measurement references. Contact between the sleeve and oarlock can change as load builds and releases. As clearance opens and closes, their angles can differ. Measuring the shaft avoids counting that relative oarlock travel as additional oar sweep.

Near the catch, the oarlock can rotate ahead of the shaft. As contact is taken up, a small change in shaft speed may appear. Catch miss measures shaft sweep before sustained loading, so it is not interchangeable with an oarlock system’s slip.

At the finish, the oarlock can continue rotating after the shaft has slowed or reversed. This extra travel can contribute to a larger oarlock based wash value than the oar based finish wash + release travel. Different event thresholds also affect the comparison. Measuring at the shaft keeps these metrics tied to the oar’s movement—the same oar held by the rower and loaded by the water.
