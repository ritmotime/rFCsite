Oar

Metrics

Loaded stroke arc length (°)? — explanation

Loaded oar sweep from confirmed load engagement to the end of the loaded drive.

Power (W)? — explanation

Work per stroke divided by the complete catch-to-catch cycle time, including recovery. Requires calibrated bend and supported oar motion.

Power (W) = positive drive work (J) / stroke-cycle time (s)

Stiffness calibration scales the work and power values. A longer recovery reduces power for the same work per stroke.

PowerSplit (s/500 m)? — explanation

Effort-equivalent 500 m pace calculated from positive oar power.

PowerSplit (s/500 m) = 500 × (2.80 / power in W)1/3

GPS pace describes travel over the ground. Wind, current, hull resistance and crew movement affect how oar effort relates to boat speed.

Mid-work position (%)? — explanation

Position along the loaded stroke arc where half of the drive work has been completed. A value below 50% places more work earlier in the arc.

Peak bend position (%)? — explanation

Position along the loaded stroke arc where shaft bend reaches its peak.

Catch miss (°)? — explanation

Oar sweep from the catch to confirmed load engagement. The shaft may move and bend before sustained loading is established.

Finish wash (°)? — explanation

Oar sweep from the loaded-drive endpoint to the feather threshold. It is zero when feathering itself defines the loaded-drive endpoint.

Release travel (°)? — explanation

Oar sweep from the feather threshold to the finish reversal. It describes movement after feathering begins, separately from the end of the loaded drive.

Ratio? — explanation

Recovery time divided by drive time. A value of 2 means recovery lasts twice as long as the drive.

Work per stroke (J)? — explanation

Positive work accumulated as the loaded oar rotates through the drive. Requires the oar stiffness calibration.

Drive work (J) = ∫ moment (N·m) × dθ (radians)

Both the load and the angle through which it acts matter. A brief high peak and a longer steady pull can produce different work. Holding load without further sweep adds no sweep work.

Sweep arc (°)? — explanation

Full oar sweep from catch to finish reversal, including unloaded movement.

Drive acceleration (°/s²)? — explanation

Increase in handle rotation speed from load engagement to peak speed, divided by the elapsed time.

Power speed (m/s)? — explanation

Effort-equivalent speed calculated from oar power. Measured speed over the ground is available under GPS.

Peak rotation speed (°/s)? — explanation

Highest handle rotation speed during the drive.

Drive rotation speed (°/s)? — explanation

Mean oar sweep speed during the drive.

Curves

Bend / moment / force (° · N·m · N)? — explanation

Three views of loading through the drive: shaft bend, bending moment about the pin, and estimated handle force.

Moment (N·m) = Moment/Bend Factor (N·m/°) × bend (°)

Handle force (N) = moment (N·m) / pin-to-hand distance (m)

The Moment/Bend Factor converts measured bend to moment for the oar and sensor positions. Changing it scales moment, work and power while leaving stroke timing and measured angles unchanged. The saved inboard lever arm determines the estimated handle force.

For example, 300 N·m through a 1.0 m lever corresponds to 300 N of perpendicular force; through a 1.2 m lever it corresponds to 250 N.

The app’s Moment/Bend Factor (MBF) uses N·m/°. A saved bend factor (FBF) in J/°² uses the older coefficient: FBF = MBF × π/180; MBF = FBF × 180/π.

Oar identity, side, geometry, sensor positions, alignment and stiffness calibration must match the recording for comparable results. A report override changes that report’s calculations; the saved setup governs future recordings.

Oar speed (°/s)? — explanation

Oar sweep rotation speed through the stroke. Positive is the drive direction; negative is recovery.

Blade bottom edge depth (cm)? — explanation

Estimated vertical position of the blade’s bottom edge, using oar geometry and the feather/square transition. The squared blade extends deeper than the feathered blade.

Negative is down from the reference pin plane. The result depends on the saved geometry, supported oar inclination and boat-motion correction. Blade position, the animation’s waterline and immersed area are estimates; they do not directly measure water level or blade immersion.

Blade-tip depth (cm)? — explanation

Estimated shaft-axis blade-tip position from oar inclination, outboard length and boat-motion correction. Requires supported oar geometry and phone motion.

Negative is down from the horizontal reference plane through the pin. The phone’s hull reference and oar alignment affect this estimate. It describes blade-tip position, not directly measured water immersion; the animation’s waterline and immersed blade area are also estimates.

Horizontal sweep angle (°)? — explanation

Horizontal oar rotation with zero at the catch, showing the drive and recovery. Requires supported motion from the oar’s sensor pair.

Feather / square (−1 to +1)? — explanation

Blade orientation through the stroke: +1 is square and −1 is feathered. The transition shows when the blade rotates between these positions.

Boat

Metrics

Distance check (cm)? — explanation

Difference between the highest and lowest relative boat displacement during a complete catch-to-catch cycle. It uses the Relative boat distance curve from phone motion; GPS is not required.

Distance check (cm) = 100 × [max(x) − min(x)]

Here x is relative forward distance in metres, obtained by integrating relative boat speed and centering it around the cycle reference. This describes the range of the boat’s movement ahead of or behind that reference.

Speed check (m/s)? — explanation

Difference between the highest and lowest relative boat speed during a complete catch-to-catch cycle. It uses phone motion; GPS is not required.

Speed check (m/s) = max(u) − min(u)

Here u is relative boat speed from gravity-corrected phone acceleration, centered on the cycle’s time-weighted mean. The check measures the full range of speed variation through the stroke.

Energy-loss check (V²) (%)? — explanation

A model of speed variation based on squared fused ground speed across the complete stroke cycle, compared with steady travel at the same mean speed. It is not a direct measurement of rowing energy lost or water resistance.

For fused ground speed v(t) and cycle duration T, the time-weighted mean is:

μ = ∫₀ᵀ v(t) dt / T

Energy-loss check (%) = 100 × [∫₀ᵀ v(t)² dt − Tμ²] / (Tμ²)

Constant speed gives 0%. The calculation requires a complete supported cycle and positive mean speed. Current affects the ground-speed reference, so values should be interpreted in that context.

Set range (°)? — explanation

Difference between the highest and lowest hull roll angles during the stroke cycle.

The phone’s orientation separates gravity from acceleration. Where a saved physical boat-level reference is available, it provides the level reference. Otherwise, neighbouring complete strokes establish a neutral gravity plane, using a 20-second local reference by default.

The range shows changes in set within the stroke. A local neutral plane follows the boat’s trim and does not establish absolute hull level.

Pitch range (°)? — explanation

Difference between the highest and lowest bow-up/bow-down angles during the stroke cycle.

Phone orientation supplies hull attitude. Without a saved physical boat-level reference, the neutral plane comes from neighbouring complete strokes, using a 20-second local reference by default. This describes movement around local trim; slow trim and phone mounting tilt cannot be separated by that local reference alone.

Fused boat speed (m/s)? — explanation

Ground speed combining GPS with phone acceleration to describe changes within the stroke. Zero means zero ground speed. Valid GPS speed is used when fusion is unavailable.

Current affects the ground-speed baseline. Relative boat speed uses a different reference: deviation around the stroke cycle’s mean speed.

Fused boat split (s/500 m)? — explanation

500 m pace calculated from the displayed fused boat speed, including its GPS fallback. A valid positive speed is needed to show a split.

Heave excursion (cm)? — explanation

Estimated range of vertical hull movement through the complete stroke cycle, from gravity-corrected phone motion.

It describes relative rise and fall around the cycle reference. It does not measure absolute height above the water or the waterline.

Minimum relative boat speed (m/s)? — explanation

Lowest speed deviation relative to the stroke-cycle mean. A negative value means slower than that reference, not backward travel. Requires supported phone motion across the full cycle.

Curves

Forward acceleration (m/s²)? — explanation

Gravity-corrected acceleration along the hull, filtered for viewing. Requires a phone fixed securely to the boat.

The phone’s orientation separates gravity from boat acceleration. Integrating this motion supplies the relative boat-speed and distance curves; supported orientation and a complete stroke cycle are needed for their check values.

Relative boat speed (m/s)? — explanation

Speed variation through the stroke from gravity-corrected phone acceleration, centered on the cycle’s time-weighted mean.

Positive means faster and negative means slower than that reference. A negative value does not mean backward travel. Its full-cycle range gives Speed check; GPS is not required.

Relative boat distance (cm)? — explanation

Relative forward displacement through the complete stroke cycle, calculated by integrating relative boat speed and centering the result around the cycle reference.

The curve shows the boat ahead of or behind that reference. Its full-cycle range gives Distance check; GPS is not required.

GPS

Metrics

GPS speed (m/s)? — explanation

Speed over the ground derived from GPS positions. Zero means no ground travel. Water current affects this speed, so it differs from speed through the water.

GPS distance (m)? — explanation

Distance traveled over the ground during the recording, calculated from GPS positions. It follows the ground track, including the effect of current.

GPS pace (s/500 m)? — explanation

Time to travel 500 m at the measured GPS speed. A valid positive speed is needed to show a pace.

Piece split (s/500 m)? — explanation

Pace for the current detected piece, calculated from its elapsed time and distance.

Piece distance (m)? — explanation

Distance traveled in the current detected piece.

GPS heading (°)? — explanation

Direction of travel clockwise from true north. Requires valid GPS data and enough movement to establish a course. It describes travel direction, independent of how the phone is mounted.

Metrics

Stroke rate (spm)? — explanation

Strokes per minute measured from successive confirmed catches.

Workout time (h:mm:ss)? — explanation

Elapsed workout time.

Piece time (m:ss)? — explanation

Elapsed time in the current detected piece.

Stroke ID? — explanation

Identifier used to locate a stroke in the workout data.

Time (s)? — explanation

Elapsed recording time at the stroke’s catch.

Body

Metrics

Heart rate (bpm)? — explanation

Heart rate over the stroke interval, from a connected heart-rate source.

Calories from work (kcal)? — explanation

Estimated active calories from the measured positive oar work represented by the recording. The calculation uses the measured work before display smoothing and an assumed net efficiency.

Calories from work (kcal) = W / (4184 × η)

W is the represented measured positive work in joules, and η is the configured net efficiency. The current default is 20% (η = 0.20), an assumption rather than a measurement of your personal efficiency.

Calibrated oar work and a valid efficiency are required. Missing work remains unavailable.

Calories from heart rate (kcal)? — explanation

Estimated active calories from heart-rate reserve, bodyweight and a supplied VO₂max. For each supported interval:

r = clamp((HR − resting HR) / (maximum HR − resting HR), 0, 1)

Active calories (kcal) = r × (VO₂max − 3.5) × bodyweight (kg) / 1000 × 5 × duration (minutes)

Requires recorded heart rate, duration, bodyweight, VO₂max and configured resting and maximum heart rates. The current app defaults to 65 bpm resting and 170 bpm maximum; enter your personal values. Maximum heart rate must exceed resting heart rate; VO₂max must exceed 3.5 mL/kg/min. Missing recorded heart rate, bodyweight, VO₂max or supported duration leaves the estimate unavailable.

Heart-rate reserve approximates oxygen-uptake reserve. VO₂max is in mL/kg/min; 3.5 mL/kg/min is an assumed resting oxygen uptake, and 5 kcal per litre of oxygen is an approximate energy conversion. Subtracting the resting oxygen level makes this an active-energy estimate.

LOAD · MOVEMENT · EFFORT

Rowing physics

Shaft bend describes loading. The force, its lever arm and the oar’s rotation determine how that loading becomes work and power.

Load and movement

At the catch, the shaft can bend before the oar has swept through much angle. There is load, but little sweep work has accumulated. Work increases as that load moves through the drive.

A short, sharp peak and a longer, steadier pull can reach the same maximum load while producing different amounts of work. Holding the oar under load without further sweep adds no sweep work, although shaft bending can store elastic energy.

Bend, moment and force

Force applied through a lever produces a turning effect, or moment, about the pin. Only the component of force perpendicular to the lever contributes to that turning effect.

Moment (N·m) = perpendicular handle force (N) × pin-to-hand distance (m)

Handle force (N) = moment (N·m) / pin-to-hand distance (m)

For example, 300 N·m through a 1.0 m lever corresponds to 300 N of perpendicular force. Through a 1.2 m lever, the same moment corresponds to 250 N.

Shaft stiffness

The shaft bends under load. Its stiffness, together with the positions where that bend is measured, determines the relationship between bend angle and moment. A calibrated Moment/Bend Factor expresses that relationship.

Moment (N·m) = Moment/Bend Factor (N·m/°) × bend (°)

A stiffer shaft requires more moment to produce the same bend angle.

Work and power

Work adds up the moment carried through each part of the drive. The sweep angle in this calculation is in radians.

Drive work (J) = ∫ moment (N·m) × dθ (radians)

Power (W) = positive drive work (J) / full stroke-cycle time (s)

The full stroke cycle includes drive and recovery. Work depends on both the load and the angle through which it acts; power also accounts for the time between catches.

The relationship between this effort and boat speed also depends on hull resistance, wind, current and crew movement.

Oar Kit · Assembly & Mounting

Assemble your oar kit.

Assemble each sensor in its housing, secure both mounts to the oar, then use the guided motions to align them. The same kit works with iOS and Android recording.

Assembly stepsMount on the oarSensor axes & bladeiOS: align & recordAndroid: align & recordMounting video

Parts for each sensor

Lay out the parts below. Repeat the same assembly for your second sensor.

1Fit the two clips

Start with the empty black housing. Hook the retaining flange of one yellow clip over the matching housing edge, then press the clip fully home. Fit the second clip on the opposite side, with its flange toward the housing.

Check that both clips are seated and their long strap slots are unobstructed.

2Fit the ring and sensor

Stretch the flexible ring around the outside edge of the sensor. Keep it even around the sensor rather than twisted or bunched at a corner.

Insert the sensor through the open back of the housing. Press it in evenly so the button and logo face remain visible through the front opening. Check that the ring stays seated as the sensor goes in.

3Add the curved saddle

The saddle goes on the curved back of the housing, between the sensor and the oar. The housing and saddle have matching curves, so they nest flush together. The concave outer face rests against the shaft. The snap lips face the housing, away from the shaft.

Engage one edge lip, then gently flex the opposite edge until it snaps into place. Check that both lips are engaged, the saddle sits flush with no gap, and the sensor remains seated.

4Strap it to the oar

Place the saddle against the shaft and wrap the strap around the oar. At each yellow clip, feed the strap through the slot from the inside (the oar side) to the outside. Fold each end back onto the strap on that same side and press it firmly onto itself.

The strap has hooks and loops together on one face; the other face is smooth. Keep the smooth back against the oar, then fold each end back without twisting so the two fastening faces meet.

The mount should stay put when you gently try to slide or rotate it. Check it again after feathering and squaring the oar.

Position and direction

  • Place one sensor between the handle and the oar button, and the other on the blade side of the button. Keep mounts clear of the oarlock and your hands.
  • Orient both sensors along the shaft with the offset sensor button toward the blade. That is the sensor’s −Y direction. The sensor’s +Y direction points toward the handle. With the oar held vertically, blade down, +Y points upward. Keep the button face visible.
  • Use the actual sensor positions in your oar details and stiffness setup. Moving a sensor changes the setup.
See the sensor direction reference →

Check orientation before alignment

Match the sensor axes to the blade.

Square the blade first. With the oar shaft approximately horizontal and the blade face vertical, fit both mounts with the sticker and button faces upward. Keep the offset buttons toward the blade. This puts the sensor faces approximately parallel to the horizontal drive/bend plane.

What RZ means

RZ is rotation about the sensor’s Z-axis. Z points out through the sticker face, perpendicular to the drive/bend plane. Rotation about that axis follows the oar’s sweep and bending in that plane. Feathering and squaring rotate about the shaft, approximately the Y-axis. The sign of drive rotation differs between port and starboard; the arrows below identify the axes, not a universal positive-drive direction.

Bare sensor: find the button end

Look straight at the sticker with the button nearer the bottom: +Y is up, +X is right, and +Z comes toward you. On this Ritmo sticker, the yellow curve points toward +Y; the lettering and offset button are nearer −Y.

Assembled mount: keep the same reference

The ring, cage and clips do not change the sensor axes. Keep the button and sticker visible. The USB-C port is hidden by the ring and mount hardware, so use the button end to check direction once assembled.

On the oar: face up with the blade squared

Use the same orientation for both sensors: one on each side of the oar button/collar, with +Y toward the handle and −Y toward the blade. Wrap and secure the hook-and-loop strap (Velcro) as shown in the assembly steps. Keep the mounts clear of the oarlock and your hands.

  • Blade squared: both sticker faces point up; neither mount is turned a quarter-turn around the shaft.
  • Buttons toward blade: +Y points toward the handle on both sensors.
  • Mounts secure: the saddle’s concave face rests against the shaft; its snap lips engage the housing, away from the shaft.
  • Then align: feather and square to confirm the mounts do not slip, enter the actual sensor positions, and perform the motion alignment. The sensor faces turn with the oar when it feathers.

Next · Motion Alignment

Align with a few deliberate movements.

With both mounts secure, record the still, feather/square and sweep movements. The app or Cloud Analysis uses them to learn how the sensors sit on this oar.

Use this motion alignment after mounting or repositioning a sensor. Oar stiffness calibration is a separate step that sets the Moment/Bend Factor used for moment, work and power.

Oar Kit · Sensor Direction

Place both sensor buttons toward the blade.

Use the offset button to tell which way the sensor should face along the shaft. Then let the motion alignment establish the sensor reference for your oar.

Sensor Y-axis direction

The round button is offset within the housing opening. Fit the sensor so that this button sits closer to the blade end of the oar.

  • Both sensors face the same way along the shaft. Sensor +Y points toward the handle; −Y points toward the blade. With the oar held upright, blade down, +Y points upward toward the handle. The offset button is on the −Y, blade-facing side.
  • The button and sensor face remain visible through the housing.
  • The saddle sits against the oar and the strap prevents the mount from rotating or sliding.

Repeat alignment if either sensor is removed, turns in its mount, or moves to a different position.

Check orientation before alignment

Match the sensor axes to the blade.

Square the blade first. With the oar shaft approximately horizontal and the blade face vertical, fit both mounts with the sticker and button faces upward. Keep the offset buttons toward the blade. This puts the sensor faces approximately parallel to the horizontal drive/bend plane.

What RZ means

RZ is rotation about the sensor’s Z-axis. Z points out through the sticker face, perpendicular to the drive/bend plane. Rotation about that axis follows the oar’s sweep and bending in that plane. Feathering and squaring rotate about the shaft, approximately the Y-axis. The sign of drive rotation differs between port and starboard; the arrows below identify the axes, not a universal positive-drive direction.

Bare sensor: find the button end

Look straight at the sticker with the button nearer the bottom: +Y is up, +X is right, and +Z comes toward you. On this Ritmo sticker, the yellow curve points toward +Y; the lettering and offset button are nearer −Y.

Assembled mount: keep the same reference

The ring, cage and clips do not change the sensor axes. Keep the button and sticker visible. The USB-C port is hidden by the ring and mount hardware, so use the button end to check direction once assembled.

On the oar: face up with the blade squared

Use the same orientation for both sensors: one on each side of the oar button/collar, with +Y toward the handle and −Y toward the blade. Wrap and secure the hook-and-loop strap (Velcro) as shown in the assembly steps. Keep the mounts clear of the oarlock and your hands.

  • Blade squared: both sticker faces point up; neither mount is turned a quarter-turn around the shaft.
  • Buttons toward blade: +Y points toward the handle on both sensors.
  • Mounts secure: the saddle’s concave face rests against the shaft; its snap lips engage the housing, away from the shaft.
  • Then align: feather and square to confirm the mounts do not slip, enter the actual sensor positions, and perform the motion alignment. The sensor faces turn with the oar when it feathers.

Finish with motion alignment

The mounting check gets the sensors facing the right way. The alignment recording uses deliberate oar movements to account for the remaining mounting angles and resting sensor offsets.

Align your oar sensors.

Four required movements establish the sensor reference. An optional squared float can then save a water reference with the oar.

Before you start

  1. Choose the correct saved oar in Connect Oar and connect its sensors.
  2. Check Port/Starboard, Scull/Sweep and mounting positions in Oar Settings.
  3. Open Align Sensors for that oar. Use the app’s prompts to start and finish each recording.

Keep the mounts secure. Moving recordings do not need a still pause at either end. Keep the blade clear of the water during the level sweeps.

The four alignment movements

  1. Bias

    Keep the blade feathered and the oar completely still for 10 seconds. Rest it on a stable support if possible.

  2. Feather and square

    Keep the shaft pointing in one direction. Feather and square the blade 5–6 times, without sweeping or lifting the oar.

  3. Feathered level sweep

    Keep the blade feathered and the shaft approximately level. Sweep back and forth through at least 90° for 5–6 repetitions.

  4. Square level sweep

    Keep the blade square and the shaft approximately level. Sweep back and forth through at least 90° for 5–6 repetitions.

Calculate the alignment after all four recordings are complete, review the result and save it to the intended oar. Recording a replacement movement makes the previous fit out of date; calculate and save again before using that replacement.

When Align both oars is offered for a connected saved pair, perform the guided movements together. Each oar keeps its own alignment. Capture any squared-float reference one oar at a time after saving the alignments.

Optional squared float

  1. Save the alignment from the four movements first.
  2. Square the blade and let it float on the water. Keep the boat level and the oar steady.
  3. Check Outboard, pin to blade tip. If you have separately measured Shaft-axis tip below water, enable that entry and enter the measurement in the displayed units. Otherwise leave it off.
  4. Confirm Blade squared and floating; boat level, then tap Start 12-second float. Remain steady for the capture.
  5. After the capture is accepted, tap Save to Oar Settings.

The float saves a reference without changing the sensor alignment or bias. Floating the blade alone does not establish an absolute water depth; the optional measured depth supplies that extra information. The saved reference travels with the oar and its workout.

What to save and when to repeat

Check that the saved alignment belongs to the physical oar and its sensor pair. Repeat alignment when a sensor moves, rotates or is replaced. A matching QR label can supply saved alignment, but verify that its mounts and sensor pair still match.

The squared-float reference is retained when alignment changes. Recheck it after the oar or boat setup changes. If a separately measured manual waterline is entered in Workout Settings, that value takes priority until cleared.

Alignment establishes the sensors’ common reference. Stiffness calibration supplies the scale for moment, work and power.

Keep recording and analysis together

The recording saves the alignment, oar settings and available float reference used for that workout. Re-analysis uses that saved setup, even if another oar is selected later.

Which value takes priority?

PrioritySourceWhat happens
1Manual Moment/Bend FactorThe entered MBF is used directly. Cperm and dimensions do not calculate the factor; saved inboard still determines handle force.
2Manual CpermThe entered stiffness constant and current dimensions calculate MBF.
3Measured CpermThe selected calibration’s stiffness constant and current dimensions calculate MBF.

These priorities apply while the corresponding manual overrides are enabled. Changing dimensions alone does not turn off an active direct MBF override. Check the displayed value, source and units before recording.

What the units mean

Moment (N·m) = MBF (N·m/°) × bend (°). Perpendicular handle-tip force (N) is moment divided by inboard length (m). Work adds positive moment × forward sweep in radians. Power divides drive work by the whole stroke cycle, including recovery.

Current iOS and report controls display Moment/Bend Factor, N·m/°. Older files may store FBF in J/degree²; the software converts that legacy value by 180/π. Do not convert a field already displaying N·m/° again.

The hull reference

In the cloud report, the default Local gravity reference measures pitch and set relative to a slowly changing neutral plane. It averages gravity directions over neighbouring complete strokes in a 20-second window. This preserves movement within each stroke while removing slow changes in the baseline.

The phone’s mounting angle is accounted for, but its forward direction must match the boat. Enter the known bow direction and phone-to-pin offsets when available; otherwise direction is estimated from the rowing pattern.

The neutral plane is a rowing reference. Slow trim changes are removed along with slow reference drift. Live app values can differ from final analysis near the beginning, end or gaps because live processing has less neighbouring data.

Change the reference under the server’s boat settings, then re-analyze. The Local gravity window can be adjusted from 5–120 seconds. The iPhone app can also use a saved boat-level reference when configured.

The replay uses a supplied physical waterline reference when available, or an estimated waterline from the model. Waterline offset lets you explore a different position. A saved squared-float reference and a physical waterline height are separate inputs.

Slip

Slip compares modelled oar movement with boat speed. It depends on synchronized data, oar dimensions, angle placement and assumptions about the water.

You can also select the extracted CSVs, keeping their filenames, or place the WitMotion TXT beside an extracted Sensor Logger folder in one outer workout ZIP. There is no need to merge rows or convert files.

6. Review the recording timezone

Before running analysis, check Recording timezone matches the location of the row, for example America/Los_Angeles. The server can read this from Sensor Logger’s metadata; an explicitly selected setting takes precedence.

Sensor Logger records absolute timestamps, while WitMotion’s date/time text normally represents local time. A wrong timezone can put the recordings hours apart. Keep Metadata.csv and the original clocks; do not move the first rows into alignment by hand.

If the report has no overlapping phone data, check the timezone and that you selected the matching workout before changing other settings.
