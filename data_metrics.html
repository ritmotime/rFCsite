Distance check, Speed check and Energy-loss check (V²) are in the GPS metric category. They are per-stroke numbers: to inspect their inputs, plot Fused boat speed (GPS + IMU). Their group, piece and workout values are stroke averages.

Oar measurements need aligned oar sensors; effort values also need calibration. Boat measurements need a phone fixed to the hull. Fused speed and the three checks need supported GPS and phone motion together. Combined blade/boat estimates additionally need synchronized oar motion and geometry. See each definition for further requirements.

For a quick check in the report, click Show stroke checks in Session Overview or All Strokes. The three checks also appear in the workout, group and piece summaries and beside the selected individual stroke. Unsupported recordings retain the choices and show an unavailable value with the required-input explanation.

For a saved iOS workout, open Workout Metrics → Recalculate boat checks. The app uses that workout’s original native phone/GPS files and saved settings, updates the supported checks, and preserves the other stroke metrics. A missing source file or unsupported cycle stays unavailable. Open All Stroke Metrics in the same section to inspect individual values.

Distance, speed and energy-loss checks

These three checks use the GPS + IMU fused boat speed over one complete catch-to-catch cycle, including recovery. The comparison boat moves steadily at that cycle’s time-weighted mean speed. A positive or negative speed difference means travelling faster or slower than that comparison boat.

Core metricMeaning
Distance check · cmThe largest minus smallest accumulated distance ahead of or behind the steady comparison boat. It measures the range of relative position through the cycle, not the total distance rowed.
Speed check · m/sThe largest minus smallest speed relative to the steady comparison boat. Subtracting the same mean speed leaves the peak-to-peak speed range unchanged.
Energy-loss check (V²) · %The excess integral of squared fused speed compared with steady travel at the same mean speed, as a percentage of the steady-speed baseline. A constant speed gives 0%; a varying speed gives a positive value.

Let T be the cycle duration, v(t) the fused speed, and μ = ∫0T v(t) dt / T. Relative distance is r(t) = ∫0t [v(τ) − μ] dτ. With speed in m/s and time in seconds, distance check in centimetres is 100 × [max(r) − min(r)]; speed check in m/s is max(v − μ) − min(v − μ).

Energy-loss check = 100 × [∫0T v(t)2 dt − Tμ2] / (Tμ2). This is the requested V² model: a speed-variation index, not a measured percentage of rowing energy lost. It uses ground speed, so it does not establish resistance through water.

The calculations use supported, full-resolution fused-speed samples before display curves are reduced. A missing boundary, unsupported gap or non-positive mean speed leaves the checks unavailable. A dash is not zero. Piece, group and workout summaries show the arithmetic mean of their valid member-stroke checks. Every valid stroke contributes equally: add its check value and divide by the number of valid strokes. Each summary is labelled Average distance check, Average speed check or Average energy-loss check. These are never summed, and the summary percentage is the average of the individual stroke percentages.

Existing Speed swing and Surge excursion describe the separate IMU-relative motion estimate. They remain available under their own names and can differ from these fused-speed checks.

For example, distance checks of 20 cm and 40 cm give an average distance check of 30 cm. Energy-loss checks of 2% and 6% give 4%. A workout average uses all valid member strokes, so a two-stroke piece does not receive the same weight as a twenty-stroke piece. Missing checks are excluded, with valid-stroke coverage reported.

Aligned oar sensors supply the basic angles and timing. Moment, work, power and effort-derived pace need calibration. Handle force needs a lever arm; combined boat-corrected and blade-tip estimates need the stated phone/oar references. Experimental depth is position relative to a reference plane, not measured water immersion.

Summary-only values and aggregation

Total work is measured member-stroke work added together, shown as J or kJ according to the view. Strokes counts members; duration, piece time and rest before piece describe the selected interval. Piece and group identifiers describe membership.

Distance check, Speed check and Energy-loss check (V²) always use arithmetic means of valid member-stroke values. Their summaries use “Average” labels. An entire workout is calculated from its member strokes, not by equally averaging pieces of different sizes. These rules do not turn actual travel distance, total work or elapsed duration into averages.

Other metrics retain the aggregation appropriate to their definition: minimum/maximum fused speed use extrema; mean fused speed uses supported duration; GPS heading uses a circular mean; signed slip uses supported drive duration; deepest blade-tip position uses the minimum signed depth. The table definitions distinguish these where needed.

Recording and calculation diagnostics

Diagnostics can include the applied bend factor, observed/interpolated GPS and heart-rate provenance, load-sample counts, missing-load fractions or durations, work/power completeness and per-metric contributing-stroke counts. These explain whether a measurement is supported. Internal integration totals and support durations remain calculation details, rather than extra rowing-performance choices.

Availability is determined by the recording and the analysis version, separately for each view. A newer website cannot insert sensor samples or new calculations into an already-downloaded report; re-analyse the original workout with the updated server.

Calibration, smoothing and stored detail

The Moment/Bend Factor converts bend into moment. It changes load and effort-derived values without changing GPS travel, stroke angles, timing or rotation speeds. The current app displays MBF in N·m/°. The supplied server field Saved bend factor (FBF, J/°²) uses the older coefficient: FBF = MBF × π/180, or MBF = FBF × 180/π. A normal iOS upload carries the recorded factor; use this conversion only when entering a value manually into a field with the corresponding label and units.

Metric smoothing and curve storage are separate. Longer reports may reduce points in individual display traces; numerical metrics and original server averages are computed before that reduction. A browser-created group uses the stored traces and can therefore have approximate curves.

Keep the source workout to rerun an analysis or inspect full diagnostics. Available choices follow the version that generated the HTML and the inputs it received.
